Notifications

Play can tell you when it's your turn, when a game you are playing ends, and when someone sits down at a table of yours that is waiting for players, even when Play isn't open. You turn them on separately on each phone, tablet or computer, and choose in Settings which of the three you want; that choice applies to all your devices.

Notifications are offered only when this parlor is reached over https. If Settings has no Notifications section, this parlor doesn't offer them.

What a notification says

A notification names the game, the table's number and the other player, such as "Your turn in Reversi. Against ann. Table 12." When someone sits down, it also says what they play with, when their program said so, such as "With GameMaster 1.0.0 on a Macintosh Plus." It never includes a move, a position, a rack, a score or anything said at the table.

The message travels through your browser's push service (Google's for Chrome, Microsoft's for Edge, Mozilla's for Firefox, Apple's for Safari and for Play on an iPhone or iPad). It is encrypted for your browser, so the push service cannot read it, though it can see that a message was sent and when.

While Play is open and in front of you, nothing is sent: the page shows the news itself.

On a computer

Chrome and Edge

  1. Open Play, sign in, and choose Settings (the gear at the top right).
  2. Under Notifications, choose Turn on for this device, and then Allow when the browser asks.
  3. If the browser doesn't ask, notifications were blocked for this site earlier. Click the icon at the left of the address bar, set Notifications to Allow, reload the page and try again.

Chrome and Edge show notifications while the browser is running, even with no Play tab open. On a Mac, System Settings, Notifications must also allow Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge; on Windows, so must Settings, System, Notifications.

Firefox

  1. In Play's Settings, choose Turn on for this device, and then Allow.
  2. If Firefox doesn't ask, click the icon at the left of the address bar, remove the block on notifications, reload and try again.

Firefox shows notifications while it is running. On a Mac, System Settings, Notifications must also allow Firefox.

Safari on a Mac

  1. Safari 16 on macOS Ventura or later: in Play's Settings, choose Turn on for this device, and then Allow.
  2. If Safari doesn't ask, open Safari, Settings, Websites, Notifications, set this site to Allow, and try again.

On an Android phone or tablet

Chrome

  1. In Play's Settings, choose Turn on for this device. Chrome may first ask whether Chrome itself may send notifications; allow it, and then allow Play.
  2. If nothing is asked, tap the icon at the left of the address bar, then Permissions, and turn Notifications on. Android's Settings, Apps, Chrome, Notifications must be on as well.

You can also add Play to the home screen from Chrome's menu (Add to Home screen, or Install app); notifications work the same either way.

Firefox

  1. In Play's Settings, choose Turn on for this device, and then Allow.
  2. If Firefox doesn't ask, tap the icon at the left of the address bar and allow notifications there. Android's Settings, Apps, Firefox, Notifications must be on as well.

On an iPhone or iPad

Notifications need iOS or iPadOS 16.4 or later, and they come only to Play added to the Home Screen, not to a Safari tab.

  1. Open Play in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  2. Open Play from its new Home Screen icon. It keeps its own sign-in, so sign in there.
  3. In Play's Settings, choose Turn on for this device, and then Allow.

Later you can change how they appear, or turn them off, in the iPhone's Settings, Notifications, Play.

Turning them off

  • In Play's Settings, choose Turn off for this device. Your other devices keep theirs.
  • Untick the kinds you don't want; that applies to every device.
  • Signing out of Play also turns notifications off for that device, so a shared computer gets none for you.
  • Blocking notifications for this site in the browser, or removing Play from the Home Screen, stops them too; the server forgets that device the next time the push service says it is gone.
